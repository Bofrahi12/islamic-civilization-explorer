Malcolm X: From the Nation of Islam to Mecca

The American civil rights leader whose 1964 Hajj transformed his understanding of Islam and brotherhood.

A life of transformation

Born Malcolm Little in 1925 in Omaha, Nebraska, Malcolm X became one of the most influential African American leaders of the twentieth century. After a troubled youth and a prison sentence, he encountered the Nation of Islam while incarcerated and rose to become its most prominent spokesman.

For years he preached the Nation of Islam's teachings, which combined Black self-reliance with a theology quite distinct from mainstream Islam. His growing doubts about the movement's leadership led to a painful public break in 1964.

The Hajj of 1964

In April 1964, Malcolm X travelled to Mecca to perform the Hajj. There he prayed alongside Muslims of every colour and nationality, an experience he described in letters home as transformative. He wrote of seeing pilgrims of all races eating, sleeping, and worshipping together as equals.

After the pilgrimage he adopted the name El-Hajj Malik El-Shabazz and embraced Sunni Islam. He founded the Muslim Mosque, Inc. and the Organization of Afro-American Unity, and began speaking of human rights for all people.

He was assassinated in New York on 21 February 1965. His autobiography, written with Alex Haley, remains one of the most widely read accounts of a journey toward Islam in the modern era.
