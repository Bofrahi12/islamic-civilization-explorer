Your First 7 Days Learning About Islam

8 min read · 2026-09-27 · Category: Seekers

A gentle day-by-day reading plan for your first week exploring Islam: what to read, what to watch, and how to approach the Quran with zero pressure.

How to use this week: curiosity, not pressure

You do not need to decide anything this week — not about belief, not about conversion, not about your identity. All we ask is about twenty minutes a day of honest curiosity. Treat this as a reading week, the way you would explore any great tradition before forming a judgment.

Days 1–2: the foundations — God and the Quran

Day 1: read our guides on what Islam is and who Allah is. The single most important idea to grasp is tawhid — God's absolute oneness. Then read Surah 112 (Al-Ikhlas) in translation: four short verses in which God describes Himself. Many seekers tell us this chapter alone rearranged their picture of God.

Days 3–4: the Prophet and the book's challenge

Day 3: meet Muhammad ﷺ — our guide on his life, then the harder question: did he write the Quran? We want you to see both the man and the claim about the book, because Islam stands or falls on whether the Quran is revelation. Read the evidence we present and test it the way you would test any historical claim.

Days 5–6: Jesus, purpose, and prayer

Day 5: read our guide on whether Jesus died on the cross, alongside our guide on the purpose of life. These two questions — who is Jesus, and why am I here — are where Islam's answers differ most sharply from what you may have assumed. Sit with the difference instead of rushing past it.

Day 7: sit with it

On the seventh day, read nothing new. Instead, journal: what moved you this week? What unsettled you? What do you still need answered? The questions that remain are your real curriculum — bring them to a Muslim you trust, or keep exploring our guides on the topics that tug at you.
