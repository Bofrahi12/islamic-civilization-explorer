# Ibn Sina (Avicenna): The Prince of Physicians

He was called *al-Shaykh al-Ra'is* — "the Chief Master" — and in Europe he was known simply as Avicenna, the Prince of Physicians. Ibn Sina, born near Bukhara in 980, wrote a medical encyclopedia so authoritative that European universities were still teaching from it six hundred years later.

## A Prodigy of the Islamic East

Ibn Sina's brilliance showed itself early: by his teens he had mastered the religious sciences, logic, mathematics, and medicine, and he was treating patients while still young. He served as a physician and adviser at various courts in Persia, a life of high office punctuated by political upheaval that forced him to flee from city to city. He wrote prodigiously throughout — philosophy, astronomy, mathematics, and poetry — but it is his two great works that secured his immortality.

## The Canon of Medicine

The *Al-Qanun fi al-Tibb* (Canon of Medicine) is a vast systematic encyclopedia of the medical knowledge of its time: anatomy, physiology, diagnosis, pharmacology, and surgery, organized with a logician's clarity. It described contagious diseases, the importance of clinical trials of new drugs, quarantine, and the role of environment in health. Translated into Latin in the 12th century, the Canon became the standard medical textbook at universities from Montpellier to Padua, remaining in use well into the 1600s — an astonishing run for any scientific work.

## The Philosopher

Ibn Sina was equally towering as a philosopher. His *Kitab al-Shifa* (Book of Healing — "healing" of the soul through knowledge) synthesized Aristotelian philosophy with Islamic thought, developing influential ideas about the nature of existence, the soul, and God. His philosophical writings shaped thinkers in the Islamic world, Jewish philosophy — Maimonides engaged deeply with him — and Christian scholasticism, where Thomas Aquinas both drew on and argued against him.

## Key takeaways

- Ibn Sina's *Canon of Medicine* organized all known medical knowledge into a single systematic work used in Europe for some six centuries.
- The Canon addressed contagion, drug testing, quarantine, and the link between environment and health.
- His *Book of Healing* made him one of the most influential philosophers of the Middle Ages across Islamic, Jewish, and Christian traditions.
- Known in the West as Avicenna, he remains the most famous physician of the medieval Islamic world.
