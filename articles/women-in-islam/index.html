Islam & Other Religions

Women in Islam: Faith, Dignity, and Diversity

10 min read · Category: Islam & Other Religions

Explore Quranic teachings, legal interpretation, history, and diversity without treating one Muslim experience as universal.

The central idea

The question 'What does Islam say about women?' can refer to the Quran, hadith, legal interpretation, Muslim history, modern state law, or the lived experience of women in a particular community. These are related but not identical kinds of evidence.

A closer look · 2

Our fair summary is this: the Quran presents women and men as human beings from one origin, addresses women as religious subjects, gives women specified inheritance rights, and instructs us to treat wives fairly. It also contains gender-differentiated rules whose interpretation is debated among our scholars.

What to take with you

Quran 33:35 lists believing men and women together in faith, truthfulness, patience, charity, fasting, chastity, and remembrance, promising forgiveness and reward. Quran 16:97 says that whoever does good, male or female, and believes will receive a good life and reward.

Common questions

What rights do women have in Islam?

The Quran grants women spiritual equality with men, the right to own property and earn independently, the right to education, and the right to accept or refuse marriage. These rights were revolutionary in 7th-century Arabia.

Why do Muslim women wear hijab?

Muslim women who wear hijab do so as an act of obedience to God and devotion, commanded in the Quran (24:31). For most, it is a chosen expression of faith, modesty, and identity — not merely a cultural custom.

Does Islam oppress women?

Muslims distinguish between Islamic teachings, which grant women dignity and rights, and cultural practices or political abuses that contradict those teachings. Many practices blamed on Islam — such as forced marriage or denying education — are rejected by Islamic scholarship.
