Why We Believe the Quran Is from God

9 min read · 2026-09-27 · Category: Quran

The central question of Islam, answered the way Muslims answer it: preservation, the literary challenge, coherence, and an invitation to test the claim yourself.

The question everything turns on

Every claim Islam makes stands or falls on one question: is the Quran truly revelation from God, or did a man compose it? We do not ask you to take our word for it — we ask you to examine the evidence the way you would examine any great historical claim, and then decide.

Preserved beyond what history usually allows

Our first reason is preservation. The Quran was memorized by thousands in the Prophet's ﷺ lifetime, written down under his supervision, and compiled into a single text within about twenty years of his death. Today millions memorize the identical Arabic text worldwide, and early manuscripts — like the Birmingham folios, radiocarbon-dated to his lifetime — match what we recite.

The literary challenge, still unanswered

The Quran's boldest claim is also its most testable: it challenges doubters to produce a single chapter like it (2:23) — and in fourteen centuries, we believe, no one has. This is not a claim about beautiful language in the abstract. The Quran arrived through a man who, by our sources and by the Quran's own statement, had not read a book or written with his hand before it (29:48).

Coherence, knowledge, and transformative power

The Quran invites its own testing: 'Had it been from other than Allah, they would have found within it much contradiction' (4:82). Revealed over twenty-three years, in war and peace, in Makkah and Madinah, addressing law, theology, history, and the human heart — yet we find one consistent voice and message. We also point to its accurate knowledge of earlier scriptures' stories, corrected where we believe those traditions had drifted, despite the Prophet ﷺ having no formal scriptural education.

Our invitation: read it and test it

We do not ask for blind faith. The Quran repeatedly commands reflection, questioning, and the use of reason — and it promises guidance to the sincere seeker: 'Those who strive for Us — We will surely guide them to Our paths' (29:69). Pick up a translation, read with an open mind, and watch how the book speaks to you directly. That direct address is itself part of the experience Muslims describe.

Common questions

Why do Muslims believe the Quran is from God?

Muslims point to several converging reasons: its remarkable preservation through memorization and manuscripts, its literary inimitability despite coming through an unlettered prophet, its internal consistency across 23 years of revelation, and its transformative impact on history and individual lives.

What is the Quran's challenge to doubters?

The Quran challenges skeptics to produce even a single chapter like it (2:23). Muslims hold that in fourteen centuries no one has met this challenge — striking, given it came through a man who had not read or written a book before revelation (29:48).

Is belief in the Quran blind faith?

Muslims say no: the Quran repeatedly commands reflection, questioning, and the use of reason, and invites its own testing — 'Had it been from other than Allah, they would have found within it much contradiction' (4:82). The invitation is to read it and judge for yourself.
