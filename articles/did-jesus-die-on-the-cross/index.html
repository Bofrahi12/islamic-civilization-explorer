Jesus in Islam

Did Jesus Die on the Cross? The Islamic View

8 min read · 2026-09-27 · Category: Jesus in Islam

What Islam teaches about Jesus and the crucifixion: the Quranic account, why it matters theologically, and his promised return.

What Islam affirms about Jesus

Before we discuss where we differ, we want Christians to know how much we honor Jesus. For us he is the Messiah, born of the virgin Mary, who spoke from the cradle, healed the blind and the leper, and raised the dead — by God's permission (3:45–49). An entire chapter of our Quran is named Maryam, and her story is told with tenderness and detail (19:16–35).

The Quranic denial, precisely worded

The Quran addresses the crucifixion directly: those who claimed to have killed Jesus 'did not kill him, nor did they crucify him — but it was made to appear so to them' (4:157). God then affirms: 'Rather, God raised him to Himself' (4:158). We read this as a clear denial that Jesus died on the cross, and an affirmation that God rescued and raised him.

Why it matters theologically

For Christians, the crucifixion is the heart of salvation — atonement through Christ's sacrifice. Our theology moves differently. The Quran teaches personal accountability: 'No soul bears the burden of another' (6:164; 35:18). We do not believe humanity inherits a sin that requires a divine sacrifice to remove; we believe each person answers for their own deeds.

The return of Jesus

Our disagreement about the cross does not diminish Jesus' cosmic role in our faith — in some ways it elevates it. We believe Jesus was raised alive to God and will return before the end of time. The Prophet ﷺ said, in a hadith recorded in Sahih al-Bukhari (3448) and Sahih Muslim (155), that the son of Mary will descend as a just judge. The Quran hints at this: Jesus is 'a sign of the Hour' (43:61).

Discussing this with Christian friends

We know this is a sensitive subject, and we do not raise it to wound. Many of us have Christian friends, neighbors, and family members, and we discuss these differences with the respect the Quran commands: 'Do not argue with the People of the Book except in the best manner' (29:46). Disagreement need not mean disrespect.
