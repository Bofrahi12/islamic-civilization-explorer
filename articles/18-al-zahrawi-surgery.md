# Al-Zahrawi: The Father of Surgery

Modern surgeons still use tools whose designs were first drawn a thousand years ago in Cordoba. Abu al-Qasim al-Zahrawi, court physician to the Umayyad caliphs of al-Andalus, compiled a medical encyclopedia whose surgical volume — complete with illustrations of some two hundred instruments — earned him the title "father of surgery."

## Cordoba, Jewel of al-Andalus

In the 10th century, Cordoba was one of the largest and most cultivated cities in the world, capital of a caliphate that prized learning. Al-Zahrawi (known in Latin as Albucasis) served as physician to Caliph Abd al-Rahman III and his successor, giving him access to the court's resources and a stream of patients whose cases sharpened his skill. He spent much of his long career — he died around 1013 — writing the work that would carry his name across continents.

## The Al-Tasrif Encyclopedia

Al-Zahrawi's masterpiece, *Al-Tasrif* (The Method), is a medical encyclopedia of about thirty volumes covering everything from pharmacology to diet. Its final and most famous volume is devoted entirely to surgery — the first independent surgical treatise of its kind. It describes cauterization, the treatment of fractures and dislocations, childbirth procedures, and operations on the eye, ear, and throat. Most remarkably, it includes detailed illustrations of surgical instruments: scalpels, forceps, hooks, saws, and catheters, many of which al-Zahrawi designed himself. He also wrote about anesthesia by inhalation and oral means, and described techniques for reducing dislocations and setting fractures that remained standard practice for generations.

## A Legacy in Steel

Translated into Latin in 12th-century Toledo, the surgical volume of *Al-Tasrif* became a standard reference in European medical schools for centuries. Surgeons across the medieval and early modern West learned their craft from instruments and techniques first set down in Cordoba. Many of the tools in a modern operating room — in their basic forms — descend from designs al-Zahrawi drew and described.

## Key takeaways

- Al-Zahrawi's *Al-Tasrif* contained the first dedicated surgical treatise in medical history, with illustrated instruments.
- He personally designed many of the roughly two hundred surgical instruments the work depicts.
- His techniques covered cauterization, fracture-setting, obstetrics, and eye and throat surgery.
- Latin translations made his work a cornerstone of European surgical training for hundreds of years.
