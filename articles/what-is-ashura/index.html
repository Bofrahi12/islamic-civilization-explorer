What Is Ashura? Meaning for Sunnis and Shias

9 min read · 2026-09-27 · Category: Muslim Life

What Ashura means: the 10th of Muharram, the Sunni fast, the tragedy of Karbala, and Shia mourning — explained with respect for both memories.

The 10th of Muharram — and the fast

Ashura is the 10th day of Muharram, the first month of our Islamic calendar. For Sunni Muslims it is a day of voluntary fasting: our Prophet ﷺ said, 'Fasting the day of Ashura, I hope, expiates the sins of the previous year' (Sahih Muslim 1162).

Karbala, 61 AH

On that same date in 680 CE, Husayn — the Prophet's ﷺ beloved grandson — was killed with his family and companions at Karbala in Iraq, after refusing allegiance to the caliph Yazid. We state this soberly: it was a political massacre of the Prophet's ﷺ own household.

Shia mourning

For Shia Muslims, Ashura is the year's most solemn commemoration: mourning gatherings (majalis) retell Husayn's story, processions fill the streets, and the faithful weep openly.

Where Sunnis and Shias differ — and agree

We differ on commemoration: most Sunnis fast and do not hold mourning rituals; Shias mourn as a religious duty. We will not take sides here — both memories are sincerely held, and triumphalism helps no one.

What a non-Muslim should understand

If you meet a Muslim weeping for a man killed thirteen centuries ago, you are witnessing something profound: a faith that treats moral memory as worship. Ashura is Islam's annual lesson that conscience has a cost — and that some prices are worth paying.
