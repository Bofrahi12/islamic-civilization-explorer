What Is Umrah? The Lesser Pilgrimage

8 min read · 2026-09-27 · Category: Muslim Life

Umrah, the lesser pilgrimage: its four rites, its virtues in hadith, and why millions of Muslims undertake this voluntary journey to Mecca.

Umrah vs hajj: the lesser pilgrimage

Umrah is the 'lesser pilgrimage' — a voluntary visit to Mecca that can be performed at any time of year, unlike hajj, which is fixed to the days of Dhul-Hijjah and obligatory for those who are able.

The rites explained

Each rite carries meaning for us: ihram strips away status; tawaf centers the heart on God; sa'i remembers Hajar's trust; the haircut marks renewal — a small death of the old self. Nothing here is decoration.

Virtues in hadith

Our Prophet ﷺ said, 'Umrah to the next umrah expiates whatever sins came between them' (Sahih al-Bukhari 1773) — we understand this as God's mercy wiping the slate for the sincere pilgrim.

The practical picture

Most pilgrims combine umrah with visiting Medina, the Prophet's ﷺ city — praying in his mosque, greeting him at his burial place, visiting the first mosque of Islam at Quba.

Why millions go

Nobody is obligated to perform umrah, yet millions save for years to go — because for us, standing before the Kaaba is a longing, not a duty.

References
