# Ibn Battuta: The Greatest Traveler of the Middle Ages

In 1325, a 21-year-old scholar left his native Tangier intending to make the pilgrimage to Mecca. He did not return home for nearly thirty years. By the time Ibn Battuta finally came back to Morocco, he had traveled farther than any other explorer of the pre-modern world — and his account of those journeys remains one of the great travel books of all time.

## Thirty Years on the Road

Ibn Battuta's travels carried him across a vast sweep of the medieval world: North Africa and Egypt, the Arabian Peninsula, Persia, Anatolia, Central Asia, India, the Maldives, China, the East African coast, and the West African empire of Mali. He sailed the Indian Ocean and the South China Sea, crossed the Sahara, and walked the steppes of Central Asia. In total he covered on the order of 120,000 kilometers — several times the distance traveled by Marco Polo.

## Qadi of Delhi, Guest of Mali

Trained as a scholar of Islamic law, Ibn Battuta repeatedly found employment as a judge (*qadi*) and envoy at the courts he visited. He spent years in India in the service of the Delhi sultan Muhammad ibn Tughluq, who sent him as an ambassador toward China. Late in his travels he journeyed south across the Sahara to the Mali Empire, visiting Timbuktu and Gao and recording vivid descriptions of West African society, trade, and customs — among the earliest detailed outside accounts of the region.

## The Rihla

After his return, the Marinid sultan of Morocco commissioned the young scholar Ibn Juzayy to record Ibn Battuta's recollections. The result was the *Rihla* ("Journey") — formally *A Gift to Those Who Contemplate the Wonders of Cities and the Marvels of Traveling*. Part adventure story, part geography, part social history, it describes hundreds of cities, rulers, saints, foods, and customs across three continents, and remains an irreplaceable window onto the 14th-century world.

## Key takeaways

- Born in Tangier in 1304, Ibn Battuta traveled for nearly 30 years, covering roughly 120,000 kilometers across Africa, Asia, and Europe.
- He served as a judge and envoy at royal courts, including years in the service of the Sultan of Delhi.
- His Sahara crossing produced early detailed descriptions of Timbuktu, Gao, and the Mali Empire.
- The *Rihla*, dictated after his return, is one of the most important travel narratives in world literature.
