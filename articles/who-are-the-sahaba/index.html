Who Are the Sahaba? The Prophet's ﷺ Companions

9 min read · 2026-09-27 · Category: History

Who the Sahaba were: the Prophet's ﷺ Companions, why Muslims honor them, their disagreements, and their legacy in every hadith.

Who they were

The Sahaba — the Companions — are the men and women who met our Prophet Muhammad ﷺ, believed in him, and died as Muslims. They are Islam's first generation: the people who heard the Quran as it was revealed and watched the Sunnah being lived.

Why Muslims honor them

Our Quran praises them: 'God was pleased with the foremost, the Emigrants and the Helpers, and those who followed them in goodness' (9:100). They transmitted the Quran and the hadith to us — every 'narrated by...' chain you will ever read passes through them.

The great names

Abu Bakr, the first caliph, who held the community together after the Prophet's ﷺ death; Umar, whose justice became legend; Uthman, who commissioned the standard Quran; Ali, the Prophet's ﷺ cousin and son-in-law, famed for courage and wisdom.

Their disagreements

Within decades of the Prophet's ﷺ death, the community tore itself in civil wars — the fitna — with Companions on opposing sides at battles like the Camel and Siffin. We present this factually: it happened, and it scarred us.

Their legacy

Without the Sahaba there is no Quran in your hands and no hadith in our books — they are the reason the sources survived. When you read 'narrated by Abu Hurayrah' or 'Aisha reported,' you are meeting them.

References

  • Qur'an 9:100 — God pleased with the foremost believers
  • Qur'an 3:103 — hold firmly to the rope of God together
  • Sahih al-Bukhari 2652 — the best of generations
