# Islam in China

Islam has been present in China for nearly fourteen centuries — almost as long as it has existed. Arab and Persian merchants brought the faith to Chinese ports during the Tang dynasty, and over time distinct Chinese Muslim communities took shape, most notably the Hui people. From the great mosques of the coast to the voyages of the Muslim admiral Zheng He, the story of Islam in China is one of remarkable endurance and adaptation.

## The Silk Road and the First Muslims

Muslim merchants arrived in China by both land and sea within decades of the Prophet Muhammad's death. Arab and Persian traders settled in the great port cities of Guangzhou (Canton) and Quanzhou, and overland caravans carried goods and ideas along the Silk Road through Central Asia. Chinese tradition dates the first mosque in Guangzhou to the 7th century. These early Muslim communities lived as foreign sojourners under the protection of Chinese emperors, trading silk, porcelain, spices, and later serving as valued intermediaries between China and the Islamic world.

## The Hui People

Over centuries of settlement, intermarriage, and conversion, a distinct Chinese Muslim people emerged: the Hui. Unlike most of China's ethnic minorities, the Hui are defined by religion rather than language — they speak Chinese and are culturally Chinese, but practice Islam. Numbering around ten million today, they live throughout China, with large communities in Ningxia, Gansu, and Yunnan. Hui cuisine, which avoids pork and follows halal practice, is famous across China, and Hui neighborhoods with their mosques — often built in traditional Chinese architectural style with upturned roofs — are found in cities from Xi'an to Beijing.

## Zheng He's Voyages

The most famous Chinese Muslim in history is Zheng He (1371–1433), the Ming dynasty admiral who commanded seven great naval expeditions between 1405 and 1433. His treasure fleets — some of the largest wooden ships ever built — sailed from China to Southeast Asia, India, Arabia, and the east coast of Africa, decades before European explorers rounded the Cape of Good Hope. Zheng He was born into a Muslim family in Yunnan, and his voyages projected Chinese power while strengthening ties with Muslim ports across the Indian Ocean, from Calicut to Jeddah.

## Islam in China Today

Islam in China today includes the Hui as well as Turkic-speaking peoples such as the Uyghurs of Xinjiang, whose Islamic heritage dates to the conversion of the Karakhanid khanate in the 10th century. Across the centuries, Chinese Muslims have navigated the tension between their faith and their Chinese identity, producing scholars, generals, merchants, and admirals. The great mosques of Xi'an and Quanzhou, blending Chinese and Islamic architecture, stand as monuments to this long encounter.

## Key takeaways

- Muslim merchants brought Islam to China during the Tang dynasty, nearly 1,400 years ago.
- The Hui people — Chinese-speaking Muslims numbering around ten million — are China's largest Muslim community.
- The Muslim admiral Zheng He led China's great treasure fleets across the Indian Ocean in the early 1400s.
- Chinese mosques often blend Islamic function with traditional Chinese architecture.
