What Does Islam Say About Charity?

8 min read · 2026-09-23 · Category: Muslim Life

Understand zakat, sadaqah, dignity in giving, and the Quranic emphasis on generosity without humiliation or display.

Generosity is both virtue and obligation

Our scripture presents care for people in need as a spiritual virtue — and for those of us who meet the conditions, it makes zakat a defined religious obligation. The broader word sadaqah covers voluntary charity and many forms of good action.

Giving without humiliation

Quran 2:261 uses the image of a grain producing many ears to describe the reward of spending in Allah's cause. Quran 2:264 warns us that reminders, hurtful treatment, and ostentation can undermine charity.

Zakat and its recipients

Quran 9:60 names categories that include the poor, the needy, administrators, debtors, travellers in need, and other groups in the verse. Our scholars interpret some terms and their modern applications in different ways.

Sadaqah can be more than money

Sahih Muslim 1009 gives examples of sadaqah that include reconciling people, helping someone with luggage or an animal, speaking a good word, and removing harm from a path.

No single calculation for every situation

Rates, thresholds, qualifying assets, debts, timing, and eligible uses are discussed through our jurisprudence and can differ by legal school and financial situation. The commonly cited 2.5 percent figure applies to some forms of qualifying wealth, not to every person's income or asset.
