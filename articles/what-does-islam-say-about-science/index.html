What Does Islam Say About Science?

8 min read · 2026-09-23 · Category: History

Distinguish Quranic encouragement to learn, historical scientific work in Islamic-world societies, and modern interpretation.

No single Islamic sentence about modern science

We do not have one sentence that defines science in the modern sense. Our Quran repeatedly directs us toward learning, reflection, observation, and gratitude — while presenting revelation primarily as guidance about Allah, purpose, and moral responsibility.

Reading, writing, and knowledge

Quran 96:1–5 begins with the command to read and mentions teaching by the pen. Quran 20:114 includes the prayer: 'My Lord, increase me in knowledge.'

Science in Islamic-world societies

The University of Oxford's History of Science Museum documents instruments and manuscripts from societies commonly called the Islamic world. It notes that makers came from different regions and were not all Muslim.

Why the phrase needs care

Arabic, Persian, Turkish, Andalusian, Central Asian, South Asian, African, Jewish, Christian, and Muslim actors participated in knowledge networks across different centuries. The shorthand 'Muslim contribution' can be useful, but it should not erase non-Muslim contributors or internal disagreement among us.

Scientific miracle claims

Some contemporary writers interpret Quranic passages as precise scientific predictions. Other scholars — including Muslims — criticise such readings as vulnerable to hindsight, selective translation, and the danger of treating a changing scientific theory as religious certainty.
