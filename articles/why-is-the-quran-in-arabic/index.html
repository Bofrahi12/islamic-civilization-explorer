Why Is the Quran in Arabic?

8 min read · 2026-09-27 · Category: Quran

Why the Quran is in Arabic: revelation in the people's language — and why translations, Arabic prayer, and non-Arab Muslims all fit the picture.

'An Arabic Quran'

Our Quran calls itself 'an Arabic Quran' (12:2) and explains why: 'We never sent a messenger except in the language of his people, to make things clear to them' (14:4). Revelation speaks the language of its first hearers — it had to, or it would not have been understood.

Does that exclude non-Arabs?

Not at all — and the proof is demographic: the overwhelming majority of the world's nearly two billion Muslims are not Arab. Our Quran declares that God made humanity 'into nations and tribes that you may know one another' (49:13) — diversity is divine design, not a defect.

Why translations aren't 'the Quran'

We distinguish carefully: a translation is a translation of the Quran's meanings, not the Quran itself. Every translation makes choices — of vocabulary, grammar, and interpretation — and the Quran's Arabic carries layers (rhythm, wordplay, precision) no translation fully captures.

The linguistic miracle claim

We hold a further belief: that the Quran's Arabic is inimitable — that no human composition matches it. The Quran itself issues the challenge: 'If mankind and jinn gathered to produce the like of this Quran, they could not' (17:88).

For the English reader

Start where you are: pick up a respected translation of the meanings and read — Al-Fatihah (the Opening), then short chapters like Al-Ikhlas (112). No translation is 'official' in our tradition; compare two or three and notice where they agree.
