# Al-Khwarizmi and the Birth of Algebra

Every time you solve for *x*, you are following in the footsteps of a scholar from 9th-century Baghdad. Muhammad ibn Musa al-Khwarizmi, working at the Abbasid court, wrote the book that gave algebra its name — and his own name, Latinized as *Algoritmi*, gave us the word "algorithm."

## Baghdad's House of Wisdom

Al-Khwarizmi worked in Baghdad during the reign of the Abbasid caliph al-Ma'mun, a ruler who poured enormous resources into scholarship. The city had become the intellectual capital of the world, and its great library and translation center — often called the House of Wisdom — drew scholars who rendered Greek, Persian, and Indian works into Arabic. Al-Khwarizmi stood at the center of this ferment, fluent in the mathematical traditions of several civilizations and ready to synthesize them into something new.

## The Book of "Al-Jabr"

Around 825, al-Khwarizmi completed *Kitab al-Jabr wal-Muqabala* — "The Book of Completion and Balancing." The word *al-jabr* in the title, referring to the restoration of broken parts in an equation, is the origin of our word "algebra." The book was not abstract theory for its own sake: it solved practical problems of inheritance, trade, land measurement, and taxation. Al-Khwarizmi presented systematic methods for solving linear and quadratic equations, describing step-by-step procedures — what we would now call algorithms — that any reader could follow.

## Algorithms and Indian Numerals

Al-Khwarizmi's influence traveled along two great roads. First, his name itself entered European languages: Latin translations of his work rendered "al-Khwarizmi" as *Algoritmi*, and from that came *algorism* and eventually "algorithm" — the step-by-step procedure at the heart of all computing. Second, he wrote an influential treatise explaining the Hindu-Arabic numeral system, with its decimal place value and the revolutionary concept of zero. Through Latin translations made in 12th-century Spain, these numerals gradually replaced the clumsy Roman system across Europe.

## Key takeaways

- The word "algebra" comes from *al-jabr* in the title of al-Khwarizmi's 9th-century book, written in Baghdad to solve practical problems.
- The word "algorithm" derives from the Latin form of his name, *Algoritmi*.
- He championed the Hindu-Arabic numeral system — including zero — which reached Europe through Latin translations of his work.
- His systematic, step-by-step approach to equations laid the foundations for both modern algebra and computer science.
