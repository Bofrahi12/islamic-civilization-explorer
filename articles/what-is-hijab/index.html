What Is Hijab?

9 min read · 2026-09-18 · Category: Muslim Life

Understand the Quranic vocabulary, the modern headscarf, modesty, and the diversity of Muslim interpretations and experiences.

A word with more than one meaning

Hijab is our Arabic term for barrier, screen, partition, or veil. In contemporary English it usually means the headscarf some of our women wear — but for us it also covers modest dress and conduct more broadly.

Modesty applies to men and women

Our Quran (24:30) first instructs believing men to lower their gaze and guard their chastity. The following verse gives believing women instructions about gaze, chastity, adornment, and covering.

Khimar, jilbab, and hijab in the Quran

Our Quran (24:31) uses khumur — veils or coverings — telling believing women to draw them over their chests. It uses jalabib — outer garments — in 33:59. And it uses “hijab” explicitly in 33:53 for a barrier in a specific household context.

History and regional variety

Veiling and seclusion existed in several societies before Islam — Roman, Byzantine, Mesopotamian, Persian. Our clothing practices then developed across different regions, classes, legal traditions, and political environments.

Personal meaning and freedom from stereotypes

Some of our women describe head-covering as worship, modesty, identity, family tradition, or public religious expression. Others don't wear it, or understand its obligation differently. Clothing alone can't reliably tell you a woman's faith, morality, politics, or freedom.
