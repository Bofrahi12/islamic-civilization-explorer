What Is the Black Stone?

8 min read · 2026-09-27 · Category: Muslim Life

What the Black Stone in Mecca is, why we honor it, what Umar said before it — and why kissing it is emulation of our Prophet ﷺ, not worship.

What it is

The Black Stone (al-Hajar al-Aswad) is set in the eastern corner of the Kaaba in Mecca, framed in silver. During tawaf — the circling of the Kaaba — it serves as the marker: each circuit begins and ends at the Stone. Pilgrims try to touch or kiss it if they can do so without harming others; most simply gesture toward it as they pass.

Umar's famous words

The strongest evidence that we do not worship the Stone comes from our own tradition. Umar ibn al-Khattab, the second caliph, stood before it and said words preserved in Sahih al-Bukhari (1597) and Sahih Muslim (1270): 'I know you are a stone that neither harms nor benefits. Had I not seen the Prophet ﷺ kiss you, I would not have kissed you.'

Why we honor it, then

If the Stone has no power, why kiss it? Our answer is simple: because our Prophet ﷺ did. This is the logic of the sunnah — emulation of the one we believe was God's messenger. We honor the Stone the way one might treasure a letter from a beloved: not for the paper, but for whose hand touched it.

Its history, stated factually

The Stone's history includes damage and loss, which we state plainly. In 317 AH (930 CE), the Qarmatians — a breakaway sect — attacked Mecca, killed pilgrims, and carried the Black Stone away to Bahrain. It remained there for over two decades before being returned. The Stone was also damaged by fire and broken into fragments across the centuries; the pieces visible today are held together by the silver frame.

The principle

The Quran gives us the frame for all of this: 'Righteousness is not that you turn your faces toward the east or the west, but righteousness is in one who believes in God...' (2:177). Direction, ritual, markers — these organize worship; they are not worship's substance. The substance is God-consciousness.
