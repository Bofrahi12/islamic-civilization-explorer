Laylat al-Qadr: The Night of Decree

8 min read · 2026-09-27 · Category: Muslim Life

Laylat al-Qadr, the Night of Decree: the holiest night in Islam, better than a thousand months — when it falls and how Muslims seek it.

Better than a thousand months

Laylat al-Qadr — the Night of Decree — is the night our Quran was first sent down, and our Quran devotes an entire chapter to it: 'We sent it down on the Night of Decree... The Night of Decree is better than a thousand months' (97:1–3) — a single night worth more than eighty-three years of worship.

When it is — and why it's hidden

Our Prophet ﷺ directed us to seek it in the odd nights of the last ten days of Ramadan (Sahih al-Bukhari 2017) — the 21st, 23rd, 25th, 27th, or 29th.

What Muslims do

Many of us perform i'tikaf — secluding ourselves in the mosque for the last ten days, detached from the world. We pray extra night prayers, read Quran, and make dua — and when Aisha asked the Prophet ﷺ what to say if she found the night, he taught her: 'O God, You are pardoning and love pardon, so pardon me' (Jami' al-Tirmidhi 3513).

The signs tradition mentions

Our hadith literature describes the night as calm and pleasant, neither hot nor cold, with the sun rising the next morning without sharp rays (Sahih Muslim 762).

For the seeker

Imagine a single night that can outweigh a lifetime — that is what we believe God offers, freely, every Ramadan. It tells you something about our God's mercy: He compresses eternity into an evening for whoever seeks Him.
