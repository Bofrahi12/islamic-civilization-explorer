Did Muhammad ﷺ Write the Quran?

9 min read · 2026-09-27 · Category: Quran

A fair, evidence-based look at the claim that Muhammad ﷺ authored the Quran — and the reasons we believe he received it as revelation.

The accusation, stated fairly

The claim that Muhammad ﷺ wrote the Quran himself is one of the oldest criticisms of Islam, and we think it deserves a fair hearing. Critics have suggested he composed it, borrowed it from Jewish or Christian sources, or was tutored in secret. These are serious claims, and we do not dismiss the people who make them — we simply ask that the evidence be examined.

He could not read or write

Our starting point is a fact even hostile sources of his time did not dispute: Muhammad ﷺ could not read or write. The Quran describes him as 'the unlettered prophet' (7:157–158), and states plainly: 'You did not recite any book before it, nor did you write one with your hand — otherwise the falsifiers would have had cause for doubt' (29:48). In a society where poetry was the supreme art, an illiterate merchant producing the Quran would itself be remarkable.

'A foreigner taught him'?

Another version of the charge was also current in Mecca: that a foreign craftsman or learned visitor secretly tutored him. The Quran answers this too: 'We know they say: A man teaches him. But the tongue of the one they hint at is foreign, while this is clear Arabic speech' (16:103). The Quran's Arabic — its rhetoric, its precision, its literary power — is not the product of a translated lesson.

What the first revelation looked like

If Muhammad ﷺ were an author launching a book, we would expect confidence and planning. The earliest accounts describe the opposite. In the cave of Hira, the angel pressed him and commanded 'Read' — and his answer, preserved in Sahih al-Bukhari (hadith 3), was: 'I cannot read.' He came home trembling and asked his wife Khadijah to cover him. This is the portrait of a reluctant recipient, not a scheming author.

How a seeker can test the claim

We do not ask anyone to believe on our authority. The Quran proposes its own test of consistency: 'Do they not reflect on the Quran? Had it been from other than God, they would have found in it much contradiction' (4:82). Revealed over twenty-three years, addressing war and peace, law and devotion, triumph and loss — yet holding one coherent voice and one unchanging message of divine oneness.
