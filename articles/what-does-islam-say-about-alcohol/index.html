What Does Islam Say About Alcohol?

9 min read · 2026-09-23 · Category: Common Questions

Explore Quranic guidance on intoxicants, authenticated hadith, legal interpretation, and the difference between religious norms and historical behaviour.

The mainstream religious position

In our mainstream interpretation, intoxicating drinks are prohibited for us. Our Quran presents this teaching through a sequence of passages, while hadith and later jurisprudence extend the discussion to the wider principle of intoxication.

A Quranic progression

Quran 2:219 acknowledges that intoxicants may have benefits but says their harm is greater. Quran 4:43 tells us believers not to approach prayer while intoxicated until we know what we are saying.

What the hadith adds

Sahih Muslim 2003a reports the principle that every intoxicant is khamr and every intoxicant is forbidden. We name the collection and number so you can distinguish the report from Quranic revelation.

Norm and lived history

Academic work on alcohol in Muslim societies documents poetry, courtly culture, consumption, debate, and changing enforcement across centuries. The University of Delaware's interview with historian Rudi Matthee makes the useful distinction that law and reality are not always identical.

A humane conclusion

Our sources instruct us to avoid intoxicants — while people affected by alcohol dependence may need medical, psychological, and social support rather than shame. A religious explanation should not turn addiction into a simple moral insult.
