Do Muslims Worship Muhammad?

8 min read · 2026-09-27 · Category: Islamic Beliefs

A clear answer to a sincere question: why Muslims love and honor the Prophet ﷺ deeply — and why worship belongs to Allah alone.

The short answer: no

No — and we want to be completely clear about this, because it matters more to us than almost anything else. 'Muhammad is only a messenger; messengers have passed away before him' (3:144). He ﷺ was a man — the greatest of men in our eyes — but a man, not divine.

The Prophet's ﷺ own warnings

The Prophet ﷺ warned us himself. 'Do not exaggerate in praising me as the Christians praised the son of Mary, for I am only a servant — so say: the servant of Allah and His messenger' (Bukhari 3445). We take this hadith, graded authentic, as a direct guardrail he placed around his own memory.

Love and honor are not worship

We do love him — deeply. 'Indeed, Allah and His angels send blessings upon the Prophet; O you who believe, send blessings upon him' (33:56). And he is 'a beautiful example' for us (33:21). But in our vocabulary, hubb (love) and 'ibadah (worship) are entirely different things. We love our parents without worshipping them; our love for the Prophet ﷺ is of that kind, raised to its highest degree.

The shirk safeguard

Islam's guard against deifying anyone is absolute. The Quran warns: 'If you associate others with Allah, your deeds would surely be void' (39:65) — and this warning is addressed to the Prophet ﷺ himself. If even he stood under the warning, then no one is above it.

Why the confusion is understandable

We understand why outsiders wonder. His name is in our testimony of faith and in the call to prayer; we mention him constantly. From the outside, that frequency can look like worship.
