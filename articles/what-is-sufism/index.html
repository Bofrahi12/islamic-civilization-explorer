What Is Sufism?

9 min read · 2026-09-27 · Category: History

Explore the mystical tradition of Islam: divine love, remembrance, Rumi's poetry, and the Sufi path through history.

The inward dimension of Islam

Sufism (tasawwuf in Arabic) is the mystical and spiritual tradition within our faith — a path emphasizing the heart's relationship with God: love, sincerity, humility, and constant remembrance. Where our law shapes outward conduct, Sufism attends to inward transformation.

Dhikr: the remembrance of God

The central Sufi practice is dhikr — remembrance of God through repeated names, phrases, and Quranic verses, done silently or aloud, alone or in gathering. Our Quran promises that 'in the remembrance of God do hearts find rest' (13:28).

Rumi and the poetry of divine love

The most famous Sufi voice in the West is Jalaluddin Rumi, the 13th-century Persian poet whose verses on love and longing are among the best-selling poetry in America today. His Mathnawi is often called 'the Quran in Persian' by admirers.

Orders, teachers, and community

Over centuries, Sufism organized into tariqas (orders) — communities gathered around a teacher (shaykh) and a shared discipline of prayer, remembrance, and service. Major orders like the Qadiriyya, Naqshbandiyya, and Mevlevi (the 'whirling dervishes') spread across our world.

Sufism and mainstream Islam

Sufism is not a separate religion or sect — most Sufis are Sunni (some are Shia), and Sufi scholars wrote standard works of our law and theology. Figures like Al-Ghazali, who reconciled mysticism with orthodoxy, are giants of our mainstream tradition.
