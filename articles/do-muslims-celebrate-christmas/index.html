Do Muslims Celebrate Christmas?

7 min read · 2026-09-27 · Category: Common Questions

Why don't Muslims celebrate Christmas — while loving Jesus deeply? A Muslim explains the theology, the scholarly debate on greetings, and advice for Christian friends.

Short answer: no — but with love for Jesus

We do not celebrate Christmas as a religious festival. The reason is theological, not cultural: Christmas celebrates the birth of Jesus as the divine Son of God, and our Quran's Surah Al-Ikhlas (112) is explicit — God 'neither begets nor is begotten.' We cannot affirm what we believe misdescribes God.

What Muslims actually do in December

December 25th is, for most of us, an ordinary day — work, school, the usual prayers. In Western countries, Muslim families vary: some share a family meal when relatives gather, some exchange no gifts at all, and none of it carries religious meaning for us.

Can Muslims say 'Merry Christmas'?

Here our scholars honestly differ, and we will not pretend otherwise. Many scholars permit — even encourage — kind greetings to Christian neighbors as simple courtesy, citing the Quran's command to deal justly and kindly with those who do not fight us for our faith (60:8). A greeting, they say, is manners, not theology.

What we share in winter

Strip away the theology, and the season's better values — charity, family, feeding the hungry, reflecting on the year — are ours too. Our Quran records Jesus' own words: 'Peace be upon me the day I was born, the day I die, and the day I am raised alive' (19:33). We say amen to that peace.

For Christian friends of Muslims

If you want to include your Muslim friends in December, do it with understanding rather than assumption: invite them to the meal, not the midnight mass; wish them well without expecting them to celebrate; and know that declining the religious parts is respect for their faith, not rejection of your friendship.
