# Rumi and Sufi Poetry

Seven centuries after his death, the Persian poet Jalal al-Din Rumi is still among the best-selling poets in the English-speaking world. A 13th-century Sufi mystic from Konya, in present-day Turkey, Rumi turned the longing for God into verses of astonishing beauty and universal appeal.

## From Balkh to Konya

Rumi was born in 1207 in Balkh, in what is now Afghanistan. As Mongol armies advanced, his family fled westward, eventually settling in Konya, capital of the Seljuk Sultanate of Rum in Anatolia. There Rumi became a respected scholar and preacher — until 1244, when he met the wandering mystic Shams al-Din Tabrizi. Their intense spiritual friendship transformed Rumi from a conventional theologian into an ecstatic poet. After Shams mysteriously disappeared, Rumi poured his longing into the Divan-e Shams, a vast collection of lyric poems named for his beloved friend.

## The Masnavi: A Spiritual Masterpiece

Rumi's greatest work is the Masnavi-ye Ma'navi ("Spiritual Couplets"), composed in his later years: six books containing some 25,000 rhyming couplets. Woven from stories, parables, and Quranic allusions, it explores the soul's journey toward union with God, and has been called "the Quran in Persian" for its spiritual authority. Its central theme is divine love — the idea that love, rightly understood, dissolves the ego and reunites the lover with the Beloved.

## The Whirling Dervishes

Rumi's followers founded the Mevlevi order, famed for the sama ceremony in which dervishes whirl in meditation, one hand raised to heaven and one turned toward earth. The turning dance embodies the mystic's orbit around divine truth. Rumi died in Konya in 1273; his tomb, beneath its famous turquoise dome, remains a place of pilgrimage for admirers of all faiths.

## A Universal Voice

Sufism — Islamic mysticism — produced many great poets, but none traveled as far as Rumi. His verses on love, tolerance, and the unity of being speak across religious and cultural boundaries, which is why his appeal has only grown in the modern world.

## Key takeaways

- Rumi (1207–1273) was a Sufi mystic of Konya whose meeting with Shams al-Din Tabrizi in 1244 transformed his life.
- His Masnavi — six books, about 25,000 couplets — is one of the supreme works of mystical literature.
- His followers' Mevlevi order is famed for the whirling dervishes' sama ceremony.
- His central theme is divine love as the path that dissolves the ego and unites the soul with God.
