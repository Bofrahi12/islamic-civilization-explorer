Islam & Other Religions

Do Muslims and Christians Worship the Same God?

8 min read · 2026-09-27 · Category: Islam & Other Religions

An honest, respectful answer to a sincere question: what Islam teaches about Allah, shared monotheism, and real theological differences.

Allah is simply the Arabic word for God

Let us start with language: 'Allah' is simply the Arabic word for God, used by Arabic-speaking Christians and Jews as well as by us. An Arab Christian praying in church says 'Allah' — it is not the name of a different deity.

What Islam affirms: one Creator of all

We believe that the God of Abraham, Moses, and Jesus is the same God our Prophet Muhammad ﷺ called people to worship. Our Quran's short chapter 112 defines Him: one, eternal, without offspring or equal — the absolute monotheism we call tawhid.

Where understandings genuinely differ

Honesty requires naming the real difference: mainstream Christianity understands God as Trinity — Father, Son, and Holy Spirit — while we insist God's oneness excludes divine plurality. Our Quran addresses Christians directly on this point (4:171, 5:73).

What the Quran says about Christians

Our Quran speaks of Christians with notable warmth in places: it says the nearest in affection to believers are those who say 'we are Christians,' because among them are priests and monks who are not arrogant (5:82).

An honest answer

So: do we worship the same God? Our faith's own answer leans yes — the same Abrahamic God — while insisting that God's oneness must be understood without partners or division. Many Christian theologians agree on the shared referent; others emphasize the differences.
