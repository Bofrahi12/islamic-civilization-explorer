What Is Eid? Understanding Eid al-Fitr and Eid al-Adha

8 min read · 2026-09-23 · Category: Muslim Life

Learn why Muslims celebrate two major Eids, how each festival connects with worship, and why customs vary across cultures.

Eid means a festival

In English, Eid is commonly used for our two principal festivals: Eid al-Fitr and Eid al-Adha. The word Eid refers to a recurring festival or celebration, while the two occasions have different religious settings.

Eid al-Fitr and the end of Ramadan

Eid al-Fitr means the festival of breaking the fast. It follows the month of Ramadan, which our Quran describes as the month in which the Quran was sent down as guidance and during which we believers fast.

Eid al-Adha and Hajj

Eid al-Adha is associated with the pilgrimage season and is often called the Festival of Sacrifice. Brandeis University explains its connection with the story of Abraham — peace be upon him — and the sacrifice offered in place of his son, while Quran 22 discusses pilgrimage and sacrifice in its own theological language.

Prayer, charity, and hospitality

A special congregational prayer is a central part of each Eid for us. We often visit relatives, share meals, give children gifts, and make charitable contributions. Eid al-Fitr is especially associated with charity before the festival prayer in many of our traditions.

How to greet someone

Eid Mubarak is our common greeting, meaning blessed Eid. As our non-Muslim neighbour or colleague, you can offer good wishes respectfully without needing to imitate every religious practice.
