A Christian's Guide to Exploring Islam

9 min read · 2026-09-27 · Category: Seekers

Exploring Islam from a Christian background? Start here: the shared ground, the three big differences stated honestly, and how to investigate fairly.

You are not starting from zero

If you come from a Christian background, you already share enormous ground with us: one Creator God — 'Hear, O Israel: the Lord our God, the Lord is one' (Mark 12:29) echoes our own Surah 112 — reverence for Jesus and Mary (an entire Quranic chapter is named Maryam), honor for the prophets, and belief in moral accountability before God.

The three big differences, stated plainly

First, the Trinity versus tawhid: we affirm God's absolute oneness and do not accept that God is three persons; the Quran addresses this directly (5:73). Second, the crucifixion and atonement: we believe Jesus was not killed nor crucified, but raised to God (4:157–158), and that no soul bears another's burden (6:164) — salvation comes through God's mercy and repentance, not through a redemptive death.

The questions Christians ask us first

'Do you worship Muhammad?' No — and we are emphatic about it. 'Muhammad is only a messenger' (3:144); worship belongs to God alone, and our Prophet ﷺ warned us against exaggerating his status. 'What about the Bible?' We honor the original revelation given to Moses and Jesus while holding that the Quran stands as its guardian and criterion (5:48).

How to investigate fairly

Read the Quran directly, not only books about it. Give it the same chance you would want others to give your scriptures: read whole chapters, not isolated verses pulled from debate websites. Then place the two texts' claims about Jesus side by side and ask which account is more coherent — judge the claims, not the communities.

If your heart moves: what changes and what doesn't

Here is something many Christians fear losing: you do not stop loving Jesus. We love him too — as the Messiah, born of the Virgin, a mighty prophet. Embracing Islam would deepen, not erase, your reverence for him; it reframes his nature, not his honor.
