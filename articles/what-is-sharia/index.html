What Is Sharia?

9 min read · 2026-09-27 · Category: Common Questions

A calm, factual introduction to what Sharia means in Islam, where it comes from, and how Muslims understand it in daily life.

Sharia means a path, not just a legal code

The word Sharia comes from Arabic and literally means a path — in classical usage, the path to water, the source of life itself. For us, it names the divinely guided way of living that God has laid out: how we worship Him, how we treat people fairly, and how we order our personal and communal life.

Where Sharia comes from: the Quran and Sunnah

We understand Sharia as flowing from two primary sources: the Quran, which we believe is God's revelation, and the Sunnah — the example of our Prophet Muhammad ﷺ. The Quran itself tells us that God appointed for each community 'a law and a way' (5:48).

Fiqh: the human work of interpretation

Fiqh is our scholars' human effort to understand and apply Sharia — the word literally means 'understanding.' Over centuries, our scholars developed careful methodologies for deriving rulings, weighing texts, context, and the public benefit.

What Sharia covers in daily life

Classical works of our tradition divide Sharia's concerns into worship (prayer, fasting, pilgrimage), personal conduct, family matters (marriage, inheritance), commercial ethics (fair trade, the prohibition of usury), and criminal justice. The largest share of our traditional legal writing concerns worship and daily ethics — not punishments.

Sharia and the headlines: keeping proportion

Severe criminal punishments do exist in our classical legal texts, but they come with extremely strict evidentiary conditions, and their application has varied enormously across our history and across countries. Most Muslim-majority legal systems today blend Islamic principles with civil codes in different proportions.
