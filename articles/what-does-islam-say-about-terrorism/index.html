What Does Islam Say About Terrorism?

9 min read · 2026-09-18 · Category: Common Questions

Distinguish Quranic ethics, hadith, Islamic law, and modern legal definitions when asking about terrorism.

Why the word needs definition

Our Quran doesn't provide a single modern sentence defining terrorism. Terrorism is a contemporary legal category whose boundaries differ across jurisdictions — so we must distinguish our religious texts from modern criminal-law language.

Life and limits in the Quran

Our Quran (5:32) presents the killing of one person as a grave moral matter — though its immediate wording addresses the Children of Israel. It tells us to fight those who fight us without exceeding limits (2:190), and to accept peace when the other side seeks it (8:61).

A hadith about non-combatants

Sahih al-Bukhari 3015 reports that our Prophet ﷺ forbade the killing of women and children after a woman was found killed during a military expedition. That's a hadith report, not Quranic revelation — and our jurists have discussed how its rule relates to combatant status, intention, and battlefield circumstances.

Terrorism, jihad, and war are different categories

For us, jihad is the broader term — striving, and in some contexts armed struggle. War describes organised armed conflict. Terrorism usually means violence or threats meant to intimidate or coerce, often by targeting civilians.

Avoiding collective blame

Modern terrorist organisations can selectively interpret our texts and contradict mainstream Muslim scholarship — but that's a scholarly assessment of particular movements, not a substitute for studying the texts themselves. Political repression, civil war, state violence, networks, strategy, and personal grievances also feed terrorism.
