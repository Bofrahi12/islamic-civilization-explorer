What Is the Shahada?

8 min read · 2026-09-23 · Category: Beliefs

A beginner-friendly explanation of the shahada, Islam’s testimony of faith, and the distinction between scripture, hadith, and Muslim practice.

The short definition

The shahada — also spelled shahadah — is the Arabic word for our testimony of faith. In its widely taught two-part form, we affirm that there is no deity worthy of worship except Allah, and that Muhammad ﷺ is His messenger.

What the Quran establishes

Our Quran repeatedly affirms Allah's oneness. Quran 3:18 describes Allah, the angels, and people of knowledge as bearing witness that there is no deity except Him, while Quran 47:19 instructs our Prophet ﷺ to know that there is no deity except Allah.

The hadith and the Five Pillars

Sahih Muslim 16c explicitly lists testimony to Allah's oneness and Muhammad's ﷺ messenger status as the first of five foundations on which our religion is built. It names prayer, almsgiving, pilgrimage, and fasting as the other pillars.

A beginning rather than a slogan

We generally connect the spoken testimony with conviction, intention, and a life directed toward worship and ethical practice. For us it is not a magic formula that replaces learning or character.

Respectful language for beginners

We can say it simply: the shahada is the central declaration of our faith, affirming Allah's oneness and Muhammad's ﷺ prophethood. We commonly describe it as the first of the Five Pillars.
