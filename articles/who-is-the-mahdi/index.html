Who Is the Mahdi in Islam?

8 min read · 2026-09-27 · Category: Islamic Beliefs

The Mahdi, Islam's awaited figure: what Sunnis and Shias believe, the warnings against false claimants, and why date-setting is forbidden.

The idea

The Mahdi — 'the rightly guided one' — is a figure many of us expect before the end of time: a righteous leader from our Prophet's ﷺ family who will fill the earth with justice as it was filled with injustice. The best-known report has the Prophet ﷺ saying the Mahdi is from his progeny (Sunan Abi Dawud 4282).

The Sunni picture

In mainstream Sunni belief, the Mahdi is a future human leader — not a prophet, not divine — who will appear near the end of time, lead the Muslim community, and establish justice. He will pray alongside Jesus at his return, with Jesus leading the prayer — a detail that underscores the Mahdi's human rank.

The Shia picture

Our Shia brothers and sisters hold a more defined doctrine: the Mahdi is the twelfth Imam, Muhammad al-Mahdi, the son of the eleventh Imam, who entered occultation — hidden by God — and remains alive until God commands his return. For Twelver Shias, he is not merely expected; he is present but hidden.

Warnings against claimants

History is littered with men who claimed to be the Mahdi — from medieval uprisings to modern cults — and our Prophet ﷺ warned that deceivers and liars would come claiming his mantle. The scholarly default toward any claimant is deep skepticism.

The point of the doctrine

The Mahdi is not a date to predict but a hope to live by: that justice will ultimately triumph, and that history bends toward God's promise. Our Quran forbids date-setting for the Hour (7:187) — and the same spirit forbids Mahdi calendars.
