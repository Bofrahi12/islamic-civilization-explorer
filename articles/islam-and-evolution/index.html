What Does Islam Say About Evolution?

8 min read · 2026-09-27 · Category: Common Questions

Does Islam accept evolution? A Muslim explains what the Quran says about creation and Adam — and where Muslim scholars honestly disagree.

Why this question is so live

If you grew up around the Western debate, you may assume Islam's position is simple: creationism versus evolution, pick a side. The reality among us is more interesting — and more honest — than that. Muslims hold a spectrum of views, and our scholars have argued about them for a long time.

What the Quran says about creation

Our Quran is unambiguous about one thing: God is the Creator of everything, and humanity's origin is special. God asks Iblis, 'What prevented you from prostrating to what I created with My own two hands?' (38:75) — and describes breathing His spirit into Adam (15:29). For us, Adam is not an allegory; he is a real prophet, the father of humanity, created directly by God.

The scholarly spectrum, honestly stated

Here is where we must be frank: Muslim scholars disagree. Some contemporary scholars accept a form of divinely guided evolution for non-human life — and a few extend similar reasoning further — while firmly affirming Adam's special creation as the Quran describes it. Others reject evolutionary theory entirely, for humans and for other species alike.

Science and revelation in our tradition

Our Quran actively commands the study of nature: 'Travel through the land and observe how He began creation' (29:20). For us, investigating the natural world is not defiance of God — it is obedience to Him. That command helped fuel the Islamic golden age of science, when Muslim scholars advanced astronomy, medicine, optics, and mathematics while believing every discovery revealed God's artistry.

Advice for the seeker

If you are exploring Islam, you do not need to resolve the evolution question before taking the next step. The core of our faith is tawhid — the oneness of God — and the belief that the Quran is His revelation. Everything else, including this debate, is secondary.
