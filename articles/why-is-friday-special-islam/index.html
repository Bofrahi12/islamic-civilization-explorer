Why Is Friday Special in Islam?

7 min read · 2026-09-27 · Category: Muslim Life

Discover Jumu'ah — the Friday congregational prayer, the sermon, and why Friday is the most blessed day of the Muslim week.

The most blessed day of the week

In our tradition, Friday (Yawm al-Jumu'ah, 'the day of gathering') holds a special rank: we hold that Adam was created on a Friday, and that the Hour will come on a Friday. For us, the day's blessed status is undisputed.

Jumu'ah: the Friday prayer

Our Quran commands it directly: 'When the call to prayer is made on Friday, hasten to the remembrance of God and leave off trade' (62:9). The midday prayer is replaced by a congregational service — obligatory for adult men, encouraged for all of us.

The sermon: two khutbahs

Before the prayer, the imam delivers the khutbah — traditionally two short sermons separated by a sitting pause. The sermon praises God, invokes blessings on our Prophet ﷺ, recites Quran, and addresses the community: ethics, current events, reminders of mercy and justice.

Friday traditions

Our tradition encourages bathing (ghusl), wearing clean or fine clothes, arriving early, and listening attentively. Many of us read Surah Al-Kahf (chapter 18) on Fridays and increase blessings upon our Prophet ﷺ.

A weekly homecoming

In Muslim-majority societies, Friday structures the week: businesses pause at midday, families lunch together after prayer, the rhythm slows. In the West, we negotiate time off and long commutes to reach a mosque — a quiet weekly act of commitment.
