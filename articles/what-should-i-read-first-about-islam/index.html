What Should I Read First About Islam?

7 min read · 2026-09-27 · Category: Seekers

New to Islam and don't know where to start? A Muslim's reading path: the Quran, a biography of the Prophet ﷺ, what to avoid, and why to find a human guide.

Start with the Quran itself

Go to the source. Get a clear modern translation of the Quran — the meanings, since the Arabic original is what we recite in worship — and begin with the short surahs at the end: Al-Ikhlas (112), Al-Falaq (113), An-Nas (114), and Al-Fatihah (1). They are brief, luminous, and central.

A biography of the Prophet ﷺ

The Quran came through a man, and his life unlocks its verses. A reliable biography of our Prophet Muhammad ﷺ — his honesty before prophethood, his persecution in Mecca, his community-building in Medina, his mercy in triumph — shows you what the revelation looks like in a human life.

One good introduction — then this site's path

After the Quran and a biography, you need a map. We built this site to be one: start with 'What Is Islam,' then 'Who Is Allah,' 'What Is the Quran,' 'Jesus in Islam,' and finally 'Thinking of Becoming Muslim' — each guide names whether its claims come from the Quran, hadith, history, or scholarship.

What to avoid at first

Avoid polemics — from any side. Anti-Islam literature and triumphalist Muslim apologetics both flatten a deep tradition into talking points, and both will mislead you about what ordinary believers actually live. You want understanding first; arguments can wait.

Read with a human

Books inform, but people transform. Visit a local mosque — most hold open evenings and welcome respectful visitors — and ask your questions to a real Muslim. You will learn more in one honest conversation than in a week of scrolling.
