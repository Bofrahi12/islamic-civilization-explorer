# Fatima al-Fihri and Al-Qarawiyyin

In the year 859, a wealthy woman in the Moroccan city of Fez used her inheritance to found a mosque that would grow into one of the great centers of learning in human history. Her name was Fatima al-Fihri, and the institution she founded — Al-Qarawiyyin — is recognized as the oldest continuously operating university in the world.

## A Pious Endowment in Fez

Fatima al-Fihri was born in Kairouan, in present-day Tunisia, to a family of Arab merchants. When the family migrated to Fez, they prospered, and after the deaths of her father and husband, Fatima inherited a considerable fortune. Rather than spending it on herself, she devoted the entire sum to a single act of charity: building a mosque for the growing Muslim community of Fez. Construction began in 859 CE (245 AH), and Fatima is said to have fasted throughout the building works, dedicating the project entirely to God. Her sister Mariam founded a second mosque, Al-Andalusiyyin, nearby.

## From Mosque to University

The mosque of Al-Qarawiyyin quickly became far more than a place of prayer. Scholars gathered there to teach, and over the centuries it developed into a full-fledged university offering instruction in theology, law, grammar, logic, medicine, mathematics, and astronomy. Students and teachers came from across the Muslim world and beyond. Among the luminaries associated with the institution were the philosopher Ibn Rushd (Averroes), the Jewish philosopher Maimonides, the geographer Leo Africanus, and Ibn Khaldun himself, who studied there. Its library, rebuilt and restored in modern times, ranks among the oldest in the world.

## A Living Institution

Unlike the ancient academies of other civilizations, Al-Qarawiyyin never closed its doors. It has operated continuously for more than eleven centuries and was formally incorporated into Morocco's modern university system in the 20th century. International bodies including UNESCO and Guinness World Records recognize it as the oldest existing, continually operating educational institution in the world — a record that begins with one woman's generosity in 9th-century Fez.

## Legacy

Fatima al-Fihri's story overturns the assumption that higher education was historically a male enterprise. Her endowment created an institution that preserved and transmitted knowledge across a millennium, shaping scholars of many faiths and civilizations.

## Key takeaways

- Fatima al-Fihri founded the Al-Qarawiyyin mosque in Fez in 859 CE, funding it entirely from her inheritance.
- The mosque evolved into a major university teaching law, theology, medicine, astronomy, and more.
- Scholars linked to Al-Qarawiyyin include Ibn Rushd, Maimonides, and Ibn Khaldun.
- It is recognized as the world's oldest continuously operating university, active for over 1,160 years.
