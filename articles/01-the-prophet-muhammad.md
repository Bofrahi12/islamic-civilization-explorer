# The Prophet Muhammad and the Birth of Islam

Muhammad, born in Mecca around 570 CE, is the central figure of Islam, regarded by Muslims as the final prophet in a line stretching back to Abraham. Over twenty-three years, his revelations reshaped the religious, social, and political landscape of Arabia. His 622 migration to Medina began the Islamic calendar and founded the first Muslim community.

## Early Life in Mecca

Born into the respected Quraysh tribe, Muhammad was orphaned young and raised by his grandfather, then his uncle. He earned a reputation for honesty as a merchant and married Khadija, a wealthy widow whose caravans he had managed. Troubled by the injustice around him, he began retreating to a cave on Mount Hira outside Mecca to meditate. Around 610, during one retreat, he experienced the first of the revelations Muslims believe came from God through the angel Gabriel. These revelations, recited over the rest of his life, were later compiled as the Quran.

## Preaching and Persecution

He preached a radical message: one God, and judgment of all people by their deeds. This challenged Mecca's polytheistic traditions and the power of its shrine, the Kaaba, which drew pilgrims from across Arabia. His early followers were few — his wife Khadija, his cousin Ali, his friend Abu Bakr — but the movement grew. The Quraysh elite responded with ridicule, boycotts, and eventually violence. A group of early Muslims sought refuge with the Christian king of Abyssinia, who protected them. After Khadija's death, the pressure intensified, and Muhammad looked beyond Mecca for a safe haven.

## The Hijra and the Community of Medina

In 622, Muhammad and his followers migrated to the oasis town of Yathrib, renamed Medina ("the City"). This Hijra was such a turning point that it became year one of the Islamic calendar. In Medina, Muhammad was not only a religious leader but a statesman: he drafted an agreement, often called the Constitution of Medina, binding the city's Muslim, Jewish, and pagan tribes into one community. He built the first mosque and established practices — prayer, fasting, charity — that still define Muslim life. Mecca's leaders did not accept this rival power, and battles followed, including victory at Badr in 624 and a costly setback at Uhud in 625. A truce at Hudaybiyyah bought the young community time to grow.

## The Return to Mecca and Final Years

By 630, Muhammad's position was unassailable, and he entered Mecca with a large force — yet the conquest was nearly bloodless. He ordered the Kaaba's idols destroyed, rededicating it to one God, and granted a general amnesty to his former enemies. Tribes across Arabia sent delegations to pledge allegiance. In 632, during his final pilgrimage, he delivered the Farewell Sermon, stressing the equality of believers and the sanctity of life and property. He died in Medina later that year and was buried in his home beside the mosque, leaving no appointed successor — a question his community had to resolve at once.

## Key takeaways

- Muhammad's revelations, compiled as the Quran, founded Islam, now followed by nearly two billion people.
- The Hijra of 622 CE marks the start of the Islamic calendar and the founding of the first Muslim community.
- In Medina, he combined religious leadership with statecraft, creating laws, alliances, and institutions.
- His peaceful conquest of Mecca in 630 united Arabia under a single faith and political order.
