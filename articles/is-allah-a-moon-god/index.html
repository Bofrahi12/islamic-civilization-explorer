Is Allah a Moon God?

8 min read · 2026-09-27 · Category: Islamic Beliefs

Debunking a persistent myth: what the Quran actually says about the moon, what 'Allah' means, and where the crescent symbol really comes from.

Where the claim comes from

The 'moon god' claim was born in 20th-century polemical pamphlets, not in any university. It argues that Allah was a pre-Islamic Arabian moon deity whom Muhammad ﷺ simply promoted. No academic historian of religion — Muslim or otherwise — holds this view, because the evidence does not exist.

The Quran forbids lunar worship explicitly

The claim collapses on the Quran's own words: 'Do not prostrate to the sun or to the moon, but prostrate to Allah, who created them, if it is Him you worship' (41:37). A scripture promoting a moon god does not command its followers never to bow to the moon.

What 'Allah' actually means

'Allah' is simply the Arabic word for God — a contraction of al-ilah, 'the God.' Arab Christians pray to 'Allah' every Sunday, and Arab Bibles print 'Allah' throughout. It is not a proper name for a tribal deity; it is the definite article plus 'god': the God, the one and only.

What about the crescent?

The crescent on domes and flags is a political emblem, not a religious one. It rose to prominence under the Ottomans, centuries after the Prophet ﷺ — much as the cross on a flag does not make a nation 'cross-worshippers.' Many of the world's mosques, including the Prophet's ﷺ Mosque in Medina, carry no crescent at all.

How to answer the claim

Ask for the primary source. The claim's supporters will cite pamphlets citing pamphlets; press for an inscription, a pre-Islamic text, a peer-reviewed historian — and the trail goes cold. That is the whole debate, honestly conducted.
