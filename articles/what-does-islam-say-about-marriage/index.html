What Does Islam Say About Marriage?

10 min read · 2026-09-23 · Category: Muslim Life

Explore Quranic language about affection and mercy, consent, marriage contracts, and the diversity of Muslim family law.

Marriage as relationship and responsibility

Quran 30:21 describes spouses as a source of comfort, and says Allah placed affection and mercy between them. For us this is relational and ethical language — not a promise that every marriage will be free from conflict, or that marriage is the only meaningful life path.

Fairness and consent

Quran 4:19 instructs us to treat wives fairly and rejects treating women as property inherited against their will. Sahih Muslim 1419a reports that a previously married woman should be consulted and that a virgin's permission should be sought.

What about polygyny?

Quran 4:3 mentions two, three, or four in a discussion of justice toward orphan women, and says that fearing injustice means marrying one. It should not be reduced to the claim that our religion commands every man to have four wives.

Mahr, contracts, and culture

Our classical jurisprudence commonly describes mahr as a marriage gift or property owed to the wife. Our scholars differed over contractual conditions, relocation, additional marriage, guardianship, and enforcement.

Religious ceremony and civil law

A nikah may have religious meaning without automatically satisfying the civil marriage requirements of every country. Oxford legal scholarship shows why religious-only marriages can have different consequences for property, maintenance, inheritance, and protection.
