What Is Jihad? Meaning, Context, and Misconceptions

9 min read · 2026-09-23 · Category: Common Questions

Understand jihad as striving or struggle, including ethical effort and contested discussions of armed force, without equating it with terrorism.

The word is wider than the stereotype

Jihad is an Arabic term connected with exertion, striving, or struggle in a morally directed cause. For us it is not a synonym for terrorism, and translating it simply as 'holy war' hides important differences in language and context.

What the Quran says about coercion

Quran 2:256 says there is no compulsion in religion. This directly cautions against describing our jihad as a command to force everyone to convert — although one verse does not settle every later debate about political authority or war.

The Quran and defensive fighting

Quran 22:39 describes permission to fight being given to those who are being fought because they have been wronged. The next verse mentions people driven from their homes and places of worship — including monasteries, churches, synagogues, and mosques.

History and scholarly interpretation

Princeton historian Michael Bonner describes jihad as a complex set of doctrines and practices that changed over time, rather than as only violence or only inward spirituality. Britannica likewise distinguishes moral, verbal, ethical, and military uses.

Common errors to avoid

It is inaccurate to say our jihad only means inner struggle, just as it is inaccurate to say all jihad is terrorism. Both claims erase part of the historical vocabulary.
